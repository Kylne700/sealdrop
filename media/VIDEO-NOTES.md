# Demo video notes

`media/demo.mp4` is built locally and is **not committed** (`media/.gitignore` keeps `*.mp4`, `*.webm` and `raw/` out of git). Where it is hosted is the lead's decision; nothing was uploaded or published.

## Regenerate (one command, any narrator)

```
VOICE=<n> bash scripts/video/build.sh
```

`<n>` is the voice number in `tools/tts-samples/KEY.md` (1 piper ljspeech-high, 2 piper cori-high, 3 Kokoro `af_heart` = the default, 4 `af_bella`, 5 `am_fenrir`, 6 `bf_emma`). The default lives in one place, `scripts/video/config.mjs` (`VOICE`). The command re-voices `media/narration.md`, redraws the slides, re-cuts the footage to the new timing, re-renders with FilmCraft inside its sandbox, finishes the audio and runs every check. A different voice changes only the narration; the same recorded take is cut again. It needs `../tools/` next to the repo (TTS voices, FilmCraft; `VIDEO_TOOLS=<dir>` overrides) and the local take `media/raw/takes/<TAKE>` (`TAKE` in `config.mjs` or the environment; takes are local only, about 0.9 GB of raw material in `media/raw/`). Edit a line of `media/narration.md` and run the same command to change the words.

The three punch-in zooms need a FilmCraft `render.py` with the zoom patch, `scripts/video/render-zoom.diff` (run from the folder that holds `tools/`: `patch -p0 < sealdrop/scripts/video/render-zoom.diff`; the patch is already applied to `tools/filmcraft/render.py` here, the original is `tools/filmcraft/work/sealdrop/render.py.orig`). Without the patch the same command still builds the video, just without zooms (`plan.mjs` says so); `ZOOM=0 bash scripts/video/build.sh` does the same on purpose.

A new recording: `node scripts/video/take.mjs mock [name]` rehearses against the mock build (no RPC); `node scripts/video/take.mjs live [name]` is the real take on Tiramisu (needs the shared demo sender to hold at least 0.02 GLM and stays under 40 `arkiv_query` per hour; a take costs about 0.0052 GLM and 8 `arkiv_query`). With `SITE_URL=https://<owner>.github.io/<repo>/` in front, `live` records against that deployed site instead of a local server (no server is started; `SITE_URL` with `mock` is refused; the take stops if the link field does not start with the site address or if any page text or frame names `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` or `[::1]`, and it needs the "Key matches this link" chip on the source page). `SITE_URL=... node scripts/video/probe.mjs` checks a deployed site first and sends no RPC call. Without `SITE_URL` every script behaves as before. Whoever runs a live take on a machine shared with other live jobs wraps it in `flock /tmp/sealdrop-live.lock` (the scripts take no lock themselves).

## Listening check and black/freeze detection (final mix)

`python3 -I scripts/video/listen.py <model dir> media/demo.mp4 <out.json> ["prompt"]` transcribes the mix with faster-whisper 1.2.1 (small.en, int8 on CPU, VAD on, beam 5, word times; the model is a download kept in /tmp), `node scripts/video/asr-compare.mjs <asr.json> [<asr-with-prompt.json>]` compares it with `media/narration.md` and writes `media/raw/asr-report.txt`, and `node scripts/video/detect.mjs` runs ffmpeg `blackdetect` and `freezedetect` and writes `media/raw/detect-report.txt` (both after a build; `build.sh` keeps FilmCraft's result in `media/raw/render.json` because the real segment starts are up to 0.2 s later than the sum of the beat lengths).

- **Word error rate** against the narration (443 normalised words: compounds split, digits as words): 2.7% without a prompt (10 substituted, 1 missing, 1 extra), 3.2% with an initial prompt that names the brand words. Per story step (no prompt): 1 problem 0.0%, 2 what Sealdrop is 1.5%, 3 live demo 2.8%, 4 how it uses Arkiv 3.8%, 5 honest limits 5.3%. Per beat: 14 of 24 beats 0.0%; `s5-3` 15.0% (the spelled-out path), `s4-5` 12.5% (1 of 8 words), `s4-2` 7.7%, `s4-1` 6.7%, `receipt` 5.9%, `verify` 5.0%, `s2-3` and `send` 4.5%, `arrive` 4.2%, `create` 2.9%.
- **Words heard differently**: 10 of the 12 differences involve the word Arkiv (9 occurrences in 8 sentences): written archive (3), archiv (3) or arcive (2); in the spoken path `arkiv/friction.md` the word was dropped and "friction" came out as one token, "archivslashfriction". None of the 9 is written "Arkiv". The two others: "polls" heard as "pulls" (`arrive`) and one extra word "protocol" (`s4-2`, only without the prompt). Heard right: Sealdrop (2 of 2, written "SealDrop" and "seal drop"), Tiramisu (1 of 1), subpoena (1 of 1, with the `suppena` respelling), WebSocket (1 of 1, "web socket").
- **Arkiv: not respelled; the lead waived the brief's rule for this one word (see the end of this item).** The brief asks for a `TTS_RESPELL` entry for a word that is misheard every time; Arkiv was misheard 9 of 9 times in the final mix and has no entry, because no entry is known to be right. The voice says "AR-kiv" (Kokoro's phonemes `ˈɑːɹkɪv`); the recogniser writes "archive" for it. I voiced the eight sentences that contain the word in four spellings and transcribed each set once without a prompt (and once with the brand-word prompt): `Arkiv` (the current one) was written "arkiv" 5 and "archive" 4 of 9 times (with the prompt "arkiv" 3, "archive" 6); `Arkive` gave the same; `Arkeev` "archieve" 5 and "archive" 4 (with the prompt "arkiv" 3, "archieve" 5, "archive" 1); `archive` "archive" 9 of 9. So the current spelling is the best of those tried, and in the full mix it is still never written "Arkiv". I found no pronunciation of the name in the Arkiv docs, litepaper, brand page or blog posts, and two web searches on 2026-10-18 found none either (the only hints are that `arkiv` is the Scandinavian word for archive and that arXiv is read "archive"; neither says how this team says it), so how the team says it ("AR-kiv" or "archive") is not known. If it is "archive", add `['Arkiv', 'Archive'], ['arkiv', 'archive']` after the path entry in `TTS_RESPELL` (`scripts/video/config.mjs`; the match is case-sensitive) and rebuild. Lead's decision (2026-10-18): keep "AR-kiv". It is the plain reading of the written name, the captions on screen spell it Arkiv, and a respelling to "archive" would claim a pronunciation that no source gives. A recogniser cannot write a word that is not in its vocabulary, so for this brand word "misheard every time" says nothing about the voice.
- **Caption drift** against the ASR word times (65 captions; caption time minus heard time): median start offset -0.10 s (a caption appears 0.1 s before its first word); 62 of 65 within 0.5 s, so by the ASR's own times the 0.5 s limit is NOT met for 3 captions (0.52 to 0.63 s). ## Disclosures

- **Recording method.** Headless Chromium with `puppeteer-core`, a fresh temporary profile per run, no extensions, no logins; the desktop, terminal and the user's own browser are never captured. Frames come from the DevTools screencast (JPEG frames with renderer timestamps), used instead of Playwright's `recordVideo` so that every frame has a timestamp and the three pages (A newsroom, B source, E explorer) can be cut on one wall clock; encoded 30 fps constant frame rate, H.264 CRF 14.
- **Injected overlay.** The visible cursor and the click ripple are drawn by the recording script into the page; the app has no such code.
- **Real clicks, shortened waits.** Every action in the live part is a real click on the real app against the real chain. Chain waits are removed with jump cuts (inbox creation about 23 s, tip send about 6 s); the cut jumps from the click to the result. The explorer clip was recorded about 2.6 minutes after the tip, after the take had waited 40 s for the explorer to index the entity, and shows that same entity.
- **Two windows, one story.** The newsroom (A) and the source (B) are two separate headless pages recorded at the same time; the cut alternates between them. It is not a screen recording of one desktop.
- **Deployed site, no local address.** The demo was recorded on the live site: the inbox link on screen starts with `https://kylne700.github.io/sealdrop/#/send/` and no frame or page text names `127.0.0.1`, `localhost` or `[::1]` (the take script stops otherwise; `take.json` has `local_address_hits: 0`). The source page shows the "Key matches this link ✓" chip, because the take uses the console's own link (the "No key check" notice never appears). The end card shows `github.com/Kylne700/sealdrop` and `kylne700.github.io/sealdrop`; the second one is the address the take ran on, and an anonymous fetch of the repo page on 2026-10-18 returned the repository page (title and description) without a login.
- **Tooling beyond FilmCraft.** FilmCraft (v0.8.0, run only through `tools/filmcraft/fc-sandbox.sh`) edits: one segment per beat, captions, 0.3 s cross-dissolves, hold-to-audio, H.264/AAC export. The FilmCraft plan has no trim or speed change (spike finding) and the brief asks for a `loudnorm` finish, so ffmpeg does: trimming and transcoding the recorded footage to the narration timing, turning slide PNGs into clips, the final `loudnorm` pass with the metadata strip, the stills and the contact sheet.
- **FilmCraft patch.** Stock FilmCraft (v0.8.0) has no zoom. `scripts/video/render-zoom.diff` adds a `zoom` list per segment to `render.py` (Motion keyframes for scale and position, smoothstep easing, window kept inside the frame, refuses a blur box under a zoom); `check.py` on `work/plan-test.json` still passes (re-run 2026-10-18: ALL PASS, 12 checks, 0 FAIL, on its existing output). The 1280x720 footage is scaled up, so zoomed pictures are slightly softer than the rest.
- **Narration.** 417 words (the brief aimed at 330 to 380); a synthetic voice, Kokoro `af_heart` (Apache-2.0 weights; the author states part of the training data is synthetic; see `tools/tts-samples/KEY.md`). Pronunciation was checked with Kokoro's phonemes; only `subpoena` is respelled (`suppena`) and the path `arkiv/friction.md` is spoken as words; a listening check (above) found "Arkiv" heard as "archive" and left it. `en_US-lessac-medium` is not used (licence).
- **"Anonymous".** The app's home page says "An anonymous tip line with no server to seize" (visible in the video); the narration avoids the word on purpose, because the README limits say the RPC node and the WebSocket see the sender's IP address. The lead may want the page and the narration to agree.
- **Slides** are rendered by `scripts/video/slides.mjs` from the app's own colours, fonts and mark; the cover is `media/cover.png`.
